# Defence test suite

Headless checks on the player's defence and everything built on it: stamina, the guard, the parry
and its window, the guard break, the perfect dodge, the dash recovery, crowd hype, the uppercut
finisher's knockback, the status effects and the parry-only lock.

Everything lives in `verify_defense.gd`. It runs one mode per process, prints a `P PASS` or `P FAIL`
line per check, ends with `RESULT mode=<name> fails=<n>` and exits with that failure count.

Presses are real `InputEventKey` events and hits go through `player.receive_hit()`, so what is under
test is the same path the game uses, not a shortcut around it.

## Running it

One mode:

```sh
Godot.exe --headless --fixed-fps 60 --path . \
  --script res://art_source/defense_tests/verify_defense.gd -- mode=parry_rules
```

`--fixed-fps 60` makes game time deterministic. Three modes mash the finisher's prompt, which gates
presses on an interval in *real* seconds, so they need real time instead:

```sh
Godot.exe --headless --max-fps 60 --path . \
  --script res://art_source/defense_tests/verify_defense.gd -- mode=super_uppercut
```

Those modes are `super_uppercut`, `gamepad_mash`, `knockback`, `knockback_boss`,
`mash_tiers_live` and `scripted`. Everything else is happy either way, `kill_shove` and the
juggle modes included: they're about what the finisher does, not the mash, so they switch the gate off.

All of them, with a summary line each:

```sh
GODOT=/path/to/Godot.exe bash art_source/defense_tests/run_defense_tests.sh
```

or a subset:

```sh
GODOT=/path/to/Godot.exe bash art_source/defense_tests/run_defense_tests.sh parry_rules parry_streak status
```

The script writes each run's full output to `art_source/defense_tests/out/<mode>.log` and prints one
line per mode. `out/` is disposable.

Every run writes to that same `out/`, so two runs going at once (two agents, say) overwrite each other's
logs whenever they share a mode. Give a run a folder of its own with `OUT_DIR`, created if it isn't
there; left unset, the logs go to `out/` exactly as before:

```sh
OUT_DIR=/tmp/my_run GODOT=/path/to/Godot.exe bash art_source/defense_tests/run_defense_tests.sh "smoke fight=danny"
```

## Modes

A mode marked **(Eric)** drives Eric's real attacks, so it asserts things about his fight as well as
the player's rules; if his fight changes, these are the ones to reconcile.

Eric's pacing rework (`Scripts/EricPacing.gd`) switches his whole fight on `EricPacing.version`: V2
ships, and V1 is the fight as it was. The modes written against the old fight are pinned to V1
(`ERIC_V1_MODES` at the top of the script) whatever ships: `stagger`, `stagger_chain`, `stagger_win`,
`stagger_lose`, `knockback`, `kill_shove`, `auto_finisher`, `auto_kill`, `tells`, `grab_parry`,
`grab_block`, `parry_projectiles`, `block_eric`, `behind`, `dash_through`, `super_uppercut`,
`gamepad_mash`, `prompt_overlap`, and also `baseline`, `guard_break`, `guard_break_grab` and
`dodge_bosses`, which drive his real attacks through to V1's Downed. The rework's own modes are under
[Eric's reworked fight](#erics-reworked-fight-ericpacing-v2).

Blocking is out of the game: `PlayerDefense.BLOCKING_ENABLED` ships off, so a held guard absorbs
nothing, costs nothing and can't break on a block, and the guard is only the parry's stance, dropping
once the press's parry window is over. The parry is untouched. Every mode plays it that way except the
ones that exist to test the blocking kept behind the switch, which turn it back on for themselves
(`BLOCKING_MODES` at the top of the script): `stamina`, `block_eric`, `behind`, `blocks`, `guard_break`,
`guard_break_timeout`, `guard_break_grab` and `guard_break_lose`. `blocking=on` or `blocking=off` overrides
that for one run of any mode; the modes that met a held guard read the switch (`blocking()`), so each one
passes both ways. A guard still breaks when its bar is emptied while it is up (a status drain, Carter's
feints, `drain_stamina()`), which is how the modes about a guard break's cost break it without blocking.

#### The dash parry

The dash cancelled into the guard, behind the player's own `dash_parry` flag: off everywhere except
Eric's fight and the training room whose only exit is that fight, both of which turn it on with one
line in their `_ready`. A block press ends the dash where it is and the guard goes up in its place, so
the dash is a non-committal approach. The parry that follows is the ordinary guarded one, so a press
that is late or mashed buys a block at full stamina instead. The cancel gives up what the dash had
not paid out - its i-frames and its dodge ghost - and keeps what it is still paying, the wait for the
next dash.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `dash_parry` | | **(Eric)** the same press in the same dash leaves it running with the flag off and ends it into the guard with the flag on; a dead stop with nothing sliding on; no landing beat but the full re-dash wait, mashed dashes still 26 frames apart; the i-frames and the dodge ghost go with the cancelled dash, so his yellow charge lands, while an uncancelled dash keeps the perfect dodge over the parry; a late press and a mashed press each buy nothing but the dash's own stamina (a block at full stamina with blocking on); the cancel sets `parry_mash_lockout` exactly as a standing press does; the guarded side still decides it; the dash's stamina stands and a parry off a cancel buys no dash back; a block already held never cancels; then his real thrown sword parried out of a cancelled dash, and Josh's fight, which never turns the flag on, leaving the same press's dash alone |

### The ring: nothing leaves the player outside it

Its own file under `ring/` (`RING_MODES`), fixed frame rate, one fight a process. Any key of `SCENES` works as `fight=`, so a new fight is covered as soon as it is listed there. The floor is read off `ArenaScene`'s `wallBoundaries`, the inner faces of its four thin walls, and every case ends with the player's body on it. His boss scene is switched off for the shared cases (`PROCESS_MODE_DISABLED`), so nothing of his holds or moves them.

## Notes for whoever runs this next

- **The pause screen's timing rules.** `SceneTree.paused` is what a pause is, so anything in fight
  code that must stop with the fight has to be pausable: Timer nodes, `_physics_process`
  accumulators and node-bound tweens are, and `get_tree().create_timer(t)` is not until its second
  argument (`process_always`) is passed as false. `Engine.time_scale` is never used to pause.
- **The resume grace is 0.15 *real* seconds**, so the modes that have to see it running or run out
  (`pause_no_leak`, `pause_mash`, `pause_dialogue`, `vs_card`) run with `--max-fps 60`; under
  `--fixed-fps` a frame costs no real time and the grace would still be open at the end of the mode.
  `beam_rush_live` runs there too, for the same reason at the other end of a fight: it plays the
  loss all the way to the defeat screen, and the outro balloon's input lock is on the real clock.
- **Every fight but Jordan's opens on a boss entrance** (`*Intro`, on `BossEntrance`), which plays
  before the lines start - so a `dialogue_ended` no longer fires seconds after the scene loads.
  `load_fight()` and `load_quiet()` cut the entrance with its `skip()`, which sets the ring and starts
  the lines (what a retry does on its own), before they end the dialogue; `entrance` and `josh_layout`
  let it play. Computah's has no walk-in to cut, so it has no `finish_entrance()` and is not found. A
  held `ui_cancel` does more than `skip()`: it skips the lines and the card's build-up too.
- **The VS card plays on every fight entry**, between the pre-fight lines and the fight, and holds
  the player until it is done. `load_fight()` skips it the way a player does and then waits its own
  0.15 real second input grace out, so every other mode starts on a fight that is already running
  and reads every press it makes. Only `vs_card` lets it play.
- **One case per process where a fight ends.** `status_end`, `locked_end` and `kill_shove` take a
  `tier=` argument rather than looping, because `FightOutro` outlives the fight scene and a second
  fight in the same process meets the first one's leftovers.
- **A fight scene that can't be opened ends the run at once.** `load_fight()` and `enter_fight()` go
  through `open_scene()`, which fails the mode and prints its RESULT line if the scene won't open or
  isn't the current one within `SCENE_LOAD_FRAMES`, rather than waiting for it forever.
- **The parry window is game time.** A parry's freeze slows the game clock, so waiting a number of
  *frames* after a press no longer clears the window. `past_window()` waits for the window itself;
  use it rather than a frame count when a test wants a plain block.
- **Eric gets parked** in the modes that only exercise the player's own rules (`park_eric()`), so his
  fight can change without those modes changing with it. The modes marked **(Eric)** deliberately do
  not park him. Parking also holds his Break gauge (V2), so a run of parries can't break him mid-test.
- **Eric's V2 attacks start in the idle step** (`attack_v2()`), as his rest timer starts them in the
  game: a state entered during a physics step counts one of its wind-up's frames in the same step.
- **A temporary driver node** named `ScratchEricDriver` in his fight scene is freed on load, so a
  debugging aid left in the scene cannot steer these runs.
- **`clone_cadence` mirrors Carter.** Its `CLONE_SHOW`, `CLONE_DASH`, `CLONE_GAP` and
  `CLONE_LIGHT_OUT` are copies of `CarterStateMachine`'s `clone_show`, `clone_dash` and `clone_gap`
  and of `CarterArtLayout.CLONE_LIGHT_OUT`, **and must be changed in the same pass as those**. The
  mode reads the real values off his fight when it is in the build and fails if the copies have
  drifted, so a model of a tighter barrage than he ships cannot hide a problem; the copies are only
  what it falls back to in a build without him.
- **`beam_rush` mirrors Carter the same way.** Its `BEAM_*` and `STRIKE_*` numbers, and the
  `MESSATSU_*` ones its volleys run on, are copies of `CarterStateMachine`'s, **and must be changed in
  the same pass as those**; the mode reads the real values off his fight when it is in the build and
  fails if the copies have drifted. `STRIKE_SHOW` is at the same 0.36 s floor as `CLONE_SHOW` and
  must never go under it. The Beam Rush has **no timeout and must never grow one**: it ends on a
  count of volleys or on his Break, and a player who neither moves nor parries is meant to die to it.
- **`messatsu` mirrors Carter the same way.** Its `MESSATSU_*` timings are copies of
  `CarterStateMachine`'s `messatsu_*` exports, and `MESSATSU_HIT_WIDTH`, `MESSATSU_BACK` and
  `MESSATSU_MIN_AIM` of `CarterArtLayout`'s, **and must be changed in the same pass as those**; the
  mode reads the real values off his fight when it is in the build and fails if the copies have
  drifted. `messatsu_travel` is the Messatsu's dodge dial, not the width: the first hit lands six
  frames after the aim latches, and only a dash whose three frames all fall inside them clears it.
  `messatsu_live` stays under `--fixed-fps` for that reason: its dodge sweep counts single frames.
- **Adding a fight:** append it to `SCENES` at the top and give it a spot in `SMOKE_SPOTS`; `smoke`
  and `blocks` then work on it. New attack ids need their block cost in `BLOCK_COSTS` or their id in
  `UNBLOCKABLE`, or `blocks` will report them as unexpected.
- **A fight with a Break gauge gets a spec file**, `gauge_fights/<fight>.gd`, and the gauge modes need
  nothing else. It holds a `const SPEC` with the keys listed over `GAUGE_FIGHTS_DIR` in the script (the
  optional `guard_break_attack` and `gauge_gains` among them), and any of the optional statics listed there (`park`,
  `reset`, `force_break`, `entry_cases`, `timer_roots`, `break_feet`, `before_kill`, `after_kill`,
  `extra`), each handed the harness as `t`. `gauge_fights/mason.gd` is the smallest example and
  `gauge_fights/jordan.gd` the reference for a fight on `BossBroken` and `BossJuggled`.
- **`hold_break_gauge(boss)`** pins a boss's gauge empty and unbreakable for the rest of a run, from
  outside the gauge. A bot or a sweep that parries far more often than a player would calls it, so a
  Break in the middle of the run can't wreck its own counts.
- **The player can't be parked off the ring any more.** `PlayerScript` puts them back on the floor after
  every physics step, whatever state they are in, so a spot past a rope comes back as the nearest one on
  it. A mode that wants them out of everything's way off the ring sets `player.may_leave_ring` while they
  are there, as `greyson/plates.gd`'s `put_feet()` does for `AWAY`, and clears it after.
- **Every mash fills on `Scripts/MashCurve.gd`.** A press is worth more on an empty bar than on a
  nearly full one, and the drain rises as it fills, so a check on what one press is worth reads
  `MashCurve.gain(base, fill)` at the fill it was pressed at, never the mash's flat gain. Near the top
  a rate a little short of what a mash asks stalls there for good, so a check that waits on a failing
  rate to fill has to allow for it never filling.
